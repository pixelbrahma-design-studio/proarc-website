Becoming a Landmark: The Making of Ajman Bank Headquarters

Some buildings are designed to fit in; a headquarters is designed to be found. When Proarc began work on the Ajman Bank Headquarters — a G+M+7 corporate complex on Al-Ittihad Street, adjacent to the Etisalat building — the brief was as much about civic presence as about square footage. The bank needed a home that read, at a glance, as the centre of its world.

The defining gesture is the facade. A bold, reflective, inclined glass skin folds across the volume, generating a series of interlocking shapes that shift with the sun and the viewer's position. Across roughly 73,071 square feet of built area, the envelope does more than enclose — it performs the building's identity to the street.

A headquarters is not designed to fit in; it is designed to be found.

Behind the drama sits a disciplined plan. Floor plates are organised for the realities of a working bank — secure cores, efficient lettable depth, and a ground plane that negotiates arrival, security and dignity in a single sequence. The theatre of the exterior is earned by the calm of the interior.

Completed in 2015, the headquarters has done what landmarks are meant to do: it has become a reference point, a building people navigate by. That is the quiet test of civic architecture — not whether it photographs well, but whether it enters the everyday language of the city.

For our studio, the project remains a touchstone for how form and function can reinforce rather than compete with one another. The most ambitious gesture and the most pragmatic plan, it turns out, are best drawn by the same hand.
