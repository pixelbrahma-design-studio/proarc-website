The Rise of Mixed-Use: Rethinking Urban Fabric in the Northern Emirates

For decades, the default unit of development across the Northern Emirates was the single-use plot: a tower here, a mall there, a villa compound somewhere beyond. The result was efficient to build and exhausting to inhabit — a city you could only experience through a windscreen. Mixed-use development is the correction, and it is reshaping the fabric of Ajman and Umm Al Quwain.

Proarc's City Life projects across Al Khor, Al Tallah and Jurf were conceived around a simple proposition: bring homes, shops and services into the same few hundred metres, and the street begins to do its job again. Ground-floor retail activates the public edge; apartments above provide the footfall that keeps it alive after office hours.

Walkability is the quiet engine of value here. When daily needs sit within a short, shaded walk, residents drive less, linger more, and the businesses below them prosper. The economics follow the experience — active frontages command stronger rents and hold them through cycles.

Mixing uses also builds resilience. A development that depends on a single tenant type rises and falls with that sector; one that blends living, working and retail spreads its risk and sustains a more constant rhythm of life. For emirates growing as quickly as Ajman and UAQ, that stability matters.

The challenge for architects is choreography — stacking and stitching uses so that a delivery dock never meets a school run, and a quiet bedroom never sits above a loading bay. Get that right, and mixed-use stops being a planning category and becomes, simply, a good place to be.
